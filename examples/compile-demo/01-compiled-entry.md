## Definition

Picking a monitor comes down to **three numbers**: size, resolution and refresh rate. Ignore the marketing; these three decide whether the monitor fits the buyer.

## Key rules

### 1. Size — 24 / 27 / 32

| Size | Fits | Doesn't fit |
|------|------|-------------|
| 24" | FPS e-sports (small screen, faster aiming) | movies, office work (cramped) |
| 27" | most people — balanced for FPS, single-player, office | — |
| 32" | single-player games, streaming, spreadsheets | competitive shooters |

Curved screens: usually 34"+; immersive, but slight image distortion — personal preference.

### 2. Resolution — must match the GPU budget

Higher is sharper, but only buy what the graphics card can drive (for mainstream online games):

| GPU price | Resolution |
|-----------|------------|
| ≤ ¥2000 (e.g. RTX 3060 and below) | 1080p (1K) |
| ¥2000–4000 (e.g. RTX 4060 Ti / 5060) | 1440p (2K) |
| > ¥4000 (e.g. RTX 5070 Ti) | 4K |

### 3. Refresh rate — 160–320 Hz is the useful range

- Below 160 Hz → visible stutter; above 320 Hz → no perceptible gain for most people.
- Refresh rate follows the **bottleneck rule**: actual smoothness is limited by the *lower* of refresh rate vs game FPS.
- Refresh rate does not consume GPU power — higher is fine, but > 320 Hz is wasted.

## Usage notes

- For a beginner, check these three numbers and nothing else; this is the anti-deception filter when a seller pushes a monitor.
- Channel-promotion sentences in the source (e.g. "DM me your budget") are not knowledge and are dropped at compile time.
